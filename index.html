Abstract

Cowork agents act across files, web content, persistent memory, external tools, and reusable skills. ActBench defines behavioral safety as whether an agent's execution remains within the permissions and state changes required by a benign task. It evaluates realized risk from complete execution trajectories rather than response-level refusals.

Each adversarial case is paired with a benign case that preserves the user instruction, configuration, initial state, grading criteria, and trusted records while adding one task-reachable payload. The benchmark constructs stronger cases with reward-guided beam search and reflection-based deep probing, then verifies safety and utility using both trusted logs and LLM-based trajectory evidence.

01

Unified coverage

One benchmark spans artifacts, tools, persistent state, context pressure, and composed permissions across six execution spaces.

02

Causal evidence

A violation requires a realized prohibited effect, a validated execution path, and evidence that the operation was outside the task configuration.

03

Adaptive attacks

Failed rollouts identify the earliest broken checkpoint, allowing the payload—not the task—to be revised using trajectory feedback.

Self-evolving construction

From a reachable edit to a verified test case

1

Empirical strategy pool

Selects a scenario, target behavior, grading criteria, and an edit location that the benign trajectory actually reaches.

→
2

Reward-guided beam search

Ranks candidates by a geometric objective over mean attack evidence and retained task utility.

→
3

Reflection-based probing

Diagnoses “not observed,” “rejected,” or “failed before effect,” then revises the same payload field.

→
4

Dual evidence verification

Combines trusted state/log evidence with reconstructed trajectory evidence before accepting the case.

AGS

Attack Grading Score

Fraction of prohibited-effect criteria satisfied in an adversarial rollout. Full success requires every criterion.

UGS

Utility Grading Score

Fraction of required task-effect criteria satisfied in the matched benign rollout. Scores are criterion coverage, not probabilities.

Search objective

s(xt) = ga(xt)α · gu(xt)1−α

The geometric form prevents high attack evidence from compensating for zero task utility, and vice versa. Reported construction uses α = 0.5, beam width 3, two proposals per beam, and depth 5.

What the paper finds

  • Every tested harness remains vulnerable on at least 73.7% of malicious rollouts under the fixed model.
  • Memory poisoning and credential exposure vary most with harness context assembly and retrieval.
  • Deceptive tool invocation remains high across all six harnesses.
✓

Why dual evidence matters

ActBench does not count a suspicious payload, a blocked call, or a pre-existing state as a realized violation. Full attack success requires agreement between trusted log/state evidence and the observed trajectory path.

Install, inspect, and reproduce

The public release includes the cases, runner, scorers, mock services, harness adapters, and released trajectories. The private task-generation and attack-search pipeline is not included.

Interpretation boundary

The study is bounded by the tested models and harnesses, a fixed ASR threshold of 0.8, one verifier configuration (β = 0.4), and case-based reflection analysis. The paper calls for paired uncertainty, guarded reruns, and construction ablations before attributing average causal gains to individual components.
